Important official facts verified on 2026-05-17
-----------------------------------------------
1. The Diversity Visa program is administered by the U.S. Department of State, not by private companies.

2. The official entry and status website is https://dvprogram.state.gov/.

3. Up to approximately 55,000 diversity visas are made available annually. Do not claim 130,000 annual green card winners unless a verified official source supports that number for a specific context.

4. The Department of State published a final rule effective September 16, 2025 establishing a $1 electronic registration fee for the DV lottery program. The website pricing should state that our package price includes the $1 government DV registration fee for each entry we submit, and that we do not add it as a separate charge. Re-check the active DV instructions before each registration season.

5. For DV-2026, the registration period began on 2024-10-02 at 12:00 noon Eastern Daylight Time and ended on 2024-11-07 at 12:00 noon Eastern Standard Time.

6. As of 2026-05-17, the DV-2027 registration start date should not be assumed. The Department of State announced that it would publish the start date as soon as practicable. Do not publish a September or October countdown unless the page clearly says it is an estimated planning countdown and not an official deadline.

7. Submission of more than one entry for the same person during a registration period can disqualify all entries for that person.

8. Selection does not guarantee a visa, green card, interview, admission to the United States, or future U.S. citizenship.

9. Entrant Status Check at dvprogram.state.gov is the official way to determine selection. The Department of State does not notify selectees by mailed letter or direct "you won" email.

10. The principal applicant must generally meet the education or work experience requirement: high school education or equivalent, or two years of qualifying work experience as defined in official instructions.

Photo requirements
------------------
The website and intake form must explain and validate the core official DV digital photo requirements:

1. JPEG (.jpg) format.
2. File size equal to or less than 240 KB.
3. Square aspect ratio.
4. Exactly 600 x 600 pixels.
5. Color photo.
6. Taken within the last 6 months.
7. Plain white or off-white background.
8. Full-face view directly facing the camera.
9. Neutral facial expression with both eyes open.
10. Head size between 50 percent and 69 percent of the image height, from chin to top of head.
11. No copied/scanned driver license or official document photos.
12. No digitally enhanced or altered appearance.
13. No hats/head coverings unless worn daily for religious purposes and the full face remains visible.
14. No uniforms except religious clothing worn daily.

Photo upload UX requirements:

1. Accept only .jpg/.jpeg.
2. Check dimensions client-side before upload.
3. Check file size client-side before upload.
4. Show a clear pass/fail photo checklist.
5. Allow the client to request professional photo correction/review.
6. Store original upload plus any reviewed/corrected version.
7. Mark photo review status internally: pending, accepted, needs replacement, corrected.
8. Show official Department of State good photo examples and red-flag photo cards for common rejection risks: glasses, hats, head tilt, leaning forward/backward, busy background, shadows, overexposure, blur, and incorrect framing.
9. Preserve planning/reference notes in PHOTO_REFERENCES.md and avoid embedding unsourced third-party screenshots directly unless rights/source are confirmed.

Selfie/photo capture feature requirements:

1. Add a "Take a selfie" or "Take DV photo" feature in a future form version.
2. The feature should guide the applicant to stand in front of a real plain white or off-white background.
3. Do not depend on background removal as the default process, because official Department of State guidance says photos must not be digitally enhanced or altered to change appearance.
4. The app may crop, rotate, resize, and compress the image to meet technical requirements, as long as the person’s appearance is not retouched or changed.
5. The app should validate JPEG format, 600 x 600 pixel output, square aspect ratio, and file size equal to or less than 240 KB.
6. The app should aim for output at 200 KB or less for our workflow, while the official DV maximum is 240 KB.
7. The app should show a face alignment outline before capture.
8. The app should warn about shadows, patterns, dark backgrounds, blur, glasses, face not centered, head too large/small, or non-neutral expression.
9. Staff should review every captured/uploaded photo before final submission.
10. If selected for interview, the applicant will generally need printed 2 x 2 inch photos on photo-quality paper.
11. Initial static implementation on requirements.html:
   - Request camera access using the browser MediaDevices getUserMedia API.
   - Use a square live preview with a face alignment guide.
   - Capture to canvas at 600 x 600 pixels.
   - Export as JPEG and reduce quality until the target is under 200 KB where possible.
   - Provide a file upload fallback for browsers/devices that block camera access.
   - Keep all processing local in the browser until backend upload is implemented.
   - Show top-of-head, eye-line, chin, and shoulder framing guidance.
   - Make the capture button visually pulse once camera access is ready.
   - Add a Validate photo button that gives a clear PASS or FAIL result for the checks that can be done locally in the browser.
   - Browser validation should check 600 x 600 pixels, JPEG output, target file size, and a basic plain light background heuristic.
   - Browser validation must warn that staff review is still required for glasses, hats, expression, shadows, blur, head position, and background objects.
   - Link to official Department of State photo examples, composition template, and digital image requirements.
12. Production camera access requires HTTPS. It should work on the production GitHub Pages/custom domain once HTTPS is active, and on localhost during development.

Customer experience strategy
----------------------------
The website must improve the customer's experience compared with doing the DV entry alone. A standalone selfie tool is not enough. The service experience should feel like a guided application intake where the customer completes a few clear steps and staff handle the official submission work.

Customer mindset:
1. The customer wants to avoid confusing official forms, photo mistakes, family-member mistakes, duplicate-entry mistakes, and missed deadlines.
2. The customer is willing to pay a service fee because they want the process organized and reviewed.
3. The customer expects a short first step, clear payment instructions, a simple upload experience, and a confirmation that staff will take over after payment.

Recommended product flow:
1. Start request:
   - Name.
   - Email.
   - Phone/WhatsApp.
   - Country of birth / chargeability country.
   - Preferred language.
   - Package selection.
   - Consent and disclaimers.
2. Payment:
   - Customer pays service fee or receives secure payment instructions.
   - Direct staff communication starts after payment is matched to the request.
3. Document and photo intake:
   - Customer takes a DV photo inside the website or uploads an existing photo.
   - Customer uploads passport or government ID if required for identity verification and spelling.
   - Customer uploads any required spouse/child photos when applicable.
   - Browser validates photo size/format where possible.
   - Staff reviews every photo and asks for replacement if needed.
4. Guided DV questionnaire:
   - Customer completes a simple step-by-step questionnaire that maps to official DV entry fields.
   - The questionnaire should use plain language, progress steps, save/resume, and conditional fields.
   - Example conditional logic: if married, show spouse details; if children under 21, show child sections.
5. Staff review:
   - Staff checks spelling, dates, countries, family members, photo readiness, education/work requirement, and duplicate-entry risk.
   - Staff contacts customer through email/WhatsApp if anything is unclear.
6. Customer confirmation:
   - Before official submission, customer receives a review summary and confirms the information is accurate and authorized for submission.
7. Official submission:
   - Staff submits during the official registration window.
   - Staff sends the official confirmation number/page to the customer and stores it in the customer folder.
8. Follow-up:
   - Staff reminds customer when Entrant Status Check opens.
   - If selected, customer enters a separate selected-case support flow; this is different from initial lottery entry support.

Recommended implementation approach:
Build the public form as a short lead/payment form first, then build the full DV questionnaire as a second secure step after payment. Do not overload the homepage form with every official DV field immediately.

Alternative implementation options:
1. One long public form:
   - Pros: all information collected at once.
   - Cons: intimidating, more abandonment, higher privacy risk before payment, harder mobile experience.
   - Recommendation: avoid for first launch.
2. Short public form + secure guided questionnaire:
   - Pros: easiest customer experience, cleaner payment flow, better privacy posture, easier staff review.
   - Cons: requires more implementation work.
   - Recommendation: preferred path.
3. Downloadable questionnaire/template:
   - Pros: fastest to launch.
   - Cons: weak user experience, manual back-and-forth, customers may make formatting mistakes.
   - Recommendation: fallback only if secure web form is not ready.
4. Staff-led WhatsApp intake:
   - Pros: familiar for international customers.
   - Cons: messy records, more privacy risk, harder to validate photos/files.
   - Recommendation: offer as support channel, not the primary intake system.

The questionnaire must clearly explain that eligible spouse and children must be included according to official instructions, even if they will not immigrate with the principal applicant. Missing a required spouse or child can create serious eligibility problems if selected.
